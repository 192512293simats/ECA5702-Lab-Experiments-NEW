# ECA5702 – Nanoelectronics Laboratory

This repository contains simulation experiments for the first 10 experiments in **ECA57 – Nanoelectronics Laboratory**. The experiments cover nanoparticle light scattering and absorption, particle-size estimation using extinction spectra, quantum confinement in quantum wells and dots, advanced transistor characteristics, and electronic band structure and transport in graphene, carbon nanotubes, CNTFETs, and GFETs.

## Experiments

### 1. Simulation of Scattering and Absorption of Multi-Layer Sphere and Core–Shell Nanoparticles Using Mie-Plot
- Models homogeneous, core–shell, and multi-layer spherical nanoparticles using their dimensions, material properties, and surrounding medium.
- Simulates extinction, scattering, and absorption efficiencies over the visible and near-infrared wavelength ranges.
- Identifies the localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR) wavelength and studies its shift with shell thickness.
- Checks the relationship between extinction, scattering, and absorption efficiencies.

### 2. Particle Size Estimation Using Extinction Spectra by Mie-Plot
- Generates extinction spectra for gold nanoparticles with different radii in a fixed surrounding medium.
- Extracts spectral properties such as peak wavelength, peak extinction efficiency, and full width at half maximum (FWHM).
- Builds calibration curves relating spectral features to particle radius.
- Estimates an unknown nanoparticle radius by comparing its spectrum with simulated spectra.

### 3. Simulation of Density of States of Quantum Well Structure Using NanoHUB
- Simulates the density of states (DOS) of a quantum-well structure for different well thicknesses.
- Identifies energy subband edges and observes the step-like DOS associated with two-dimensional confinement.
- Studies how well thickness and material effective mass affect subband energies and DOS step height.
- Compares simulated results with the infinite quantum-well model.

### 4. Simulation of Energy States of Quantum Dot Using NanoHUB
- Simulates the energy states of quantum dots with different sizes and structures.
- Studies how quantum confinement affects the allowed energy levels.
- Compares energy states for different quantum-dot geometries, as specified in the experiment.
- Relates the simulated energy levels to the size-dependent behaviour of nanoscale materials.

### 5. MOSFET Scaling Analysis and Subthreshold Swing Calculation Using NanoHUB
- Studies how changes in MOSFET dimensions affect transistor characteristics.
- Analyses subthreshold swing and its relationship to transistor switching behaviour.
- Observes the effects of scaling on current, leakage, and device performance.
- Uses simulation results to understand challenges in nanoscale MOSFET design.

### 6. Simulation and Comparative Analysis of GAA-FET and Vertical MOSFET Characteristics Using NanoHUB
- Simulates the electrical characteristics of Gate-All-Around FET (GAA-FET) and vertical MOSFET devices.
- Examines the relationship between applied terminal voltages and device current.
- Compares the characteristics of the two transistor structures.
- Studies how device architecture influences channel control and transistor performance.

### 7. Simulation of V–I Characteristics of Multi-Gate FinFET Using NanoHUB
- Simulates the voltage–current (V–I) characteristics of a multi-gate FinFET.
- Studies how gate voltage affects drain current under different operating conditions.
- Observes the electrical response of a multi-gate transistor through simulation plots.
- Relates the results to the role of multi-gate structures in controlling the channel.

### 8. Simulation of Single-Electron Transistor (SET) and Tunnel FET Characteristics Using NanoHUB
- Studies the operating characteristics of a single-electron transistor (SET) and a Tunnel Field-Effect Transistor (TFET).
- Examines the role of electron transport and tunnelling in device operation.
- Observes and compares the simulated electrical characteristics of the devices.
- Introduces nanoscale transistor concepts relevant to low-power electronic applications.

### 9. Band Structure Simulation for Graphene and Carbon Nanotubes Using NanoHUB
- Simulates and examines the electronic band structure of graphene and carbon nanotubes.
- Studies energy bands and band gaps to understand their electronic properties.
- Compares metallic and semiconducting behaviour where shown by the simulated structures.
- Relates band structure to the potential use of these nanomaterials in electronic devices.

### 10. Simulation of Electronic Transport Characteristics in CNTFET and GFET Using NanoHUB
- Simulates the electronic transport characteristics of Carbon Nanotube Field-Effect Transistors (CNTFETs) and Graphene Field-Effect Transistors (GFETs).
- Examines how applied gate voltage affects current and device response.
- Compares the simulated transport behaviour of CNTFET and GFET devices.
- Studies the role of carbon-based nanomaterials in field-effect transistor technology.

## Requirements

- **Mie-Plot** for nanoparticle scattering, absorption, extinction spectra, and particle-size estimation.
- **nanoHUB account and access to the relevant simulation tools** for quantum-well, quantum-dot, transistor, band-structure, and electronic-transport experiments.
- A modern web browser and a stable internet connection for online simulation tools.
- MATLAB or Python may be useful for additional plotting, calculations, or data analysis where required by the lab manual.

Tool availability and interface names may change. Use the simulation tool and settings stated in the lab manual for each experiment.

## How to Run

1. Open the relevant simulation platform: Mie-Plot or nanoHUB, depending on the experiment.
2. Select the simulation tool specified in the lab manual.
3. Enter the material properties, device dimensions, voltage settings, wavelength range, or other required parameters.
4. Run the simulation and inspect the generated plots and numerical outputs.
5. Record the required values and compare the results with the expected trends or theoretical values in the lab manual.
6. Save the plots and results required for the lab record.
